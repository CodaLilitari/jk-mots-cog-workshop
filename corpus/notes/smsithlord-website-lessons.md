# Lessons from SM Sith Lord's Jedi Knight work

Source review: 25 October 2026. Original synthesis by Coda, collaborating with Andrei. The experiments, historical projects and published observations discussed here belong to **SM Sith Lord / COG Factory**, with the additional contributors credited on each original page. This study uses their work as an attributed research lead. It does not reproduce their COGs, geometry, artwork, audio or unpublished toolchain.

## Coverage and evidence

The review followed the public root navigation, the OpenJKDF2 Libretro site, both COG Factory generations, the current published library feed, the public status feed, five historical gallery metadata sets, and the blog's Jedi Knight category/index and linked project pages. The linked crawl acquired **111 documents without a failed request or remaining queue**. This includes 69 numbered Generation 1 project pages and 11 Generation 2 research/project pages. The 18-entry root library was screened for JK material. The live Generation 2 feed has 11 published entries: eight first-party entries and three guest projects credited to Jezz. Fourteen historical JK blog pages and selected posts were read through their public WordPress representations.

Depth varies and is recorded per source in [the source catalog](../reference/smsithlord-source-catalog.json): Generation 1 conclusions and project metadata were reviewed across the archive; the key integration pages and all Generation 2 project write-ups received page-text review. Gallery captions and data were inspected, but their images and videos were not visually evaluated. One download, P57, was acquired for an inert ZIP member/readme inspection; it contained a GOB, readme and screenshot, not the described browser generator. No downloaded executable or game content was run.

The catalog includes 59 download links from the linked crawl, nine download references in the live published feed, and explicitly lists the remaining download bodies. It also preserves external-link and unavailable-source boundaries. This is closure of the **observed public linked JK page frontier**, not proof that no unlinked, untagged, private or unpublished material exists. The root's current Home and Jerec side-scroller application shells and eight selected static JavaScript/configuration/scene sources were inspected; their browser runtimes were not evaluated. Non-JK application branches and third-party repository histories were not recursively reviewed. The Generation 1 toolchain is explicitly described as unreleased on its [toolchain page](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/toolchain.html). No private tools or author source repository were accessed.

The findings below are the site's published observations until independently checked in our own source or runtime. Its reported test counts are not our test results. Original JK, original MotS, OpenJKDF2, OpenMotS and SM Sith Lord's libretro build remain separate targets.

## Changes worth making in our tools

| Lesson | Original source | Original application to our projects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resource capacity must include implicit consumers. P68 reports that a small static COG allocation omitted stock inventory scripts, disabling shields outside the intended arena. | [P68, now obsolete](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p68-survival-arena.html), [P70](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p70-elite-arena.html) | Add a static-world capacity preflight that counts explicit rows and inventory COG demand, reports unresolved inputs, and keeps declared capacity separate from known registrations. Use independent synthetic boundary fixtures, not the author's gameplay files. |
| Shared resource files are whole replacements, not automatic patches. Partial inventory tables can silently remove stock behavior. | [P53](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p53-campaign.html), [P69](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p69-mod-menu.html) | Extend WBW package diagnostics to identify overlapping `static.jkl`, `items.dat`, `jkstrings.uni` and relevant selection/preload tables. Require explicit startup placement and target/load-order evidence; do not silently merge content or assume retail filename rules match an enhanced engine. |
| A working enhanced-engine call can still be absent or have a different contract in retail JK. | [P68](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p68-survival-arena.html), [P60](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p60-crab.html), [P22's unresolved limit experiment](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p22-coglimit.html) | Continue using measured per-target verb registries. Treat the reported held-weapon and trigonometry cases as regression leads, not permission to add unsupported verbs to the JK registry. Do not turn P22 into a settled numeric COG limit. |
| Static presentation, timers and heap-derived caches need an explicit lifecycle contract. | [P67](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p67-battle-tactics-3.html), [P69](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p69-mod-menu.html) | Make save/load, cross-level load, restart, death, respawn and teardown distinct acceptance cases. Reconstruct disposable visual state from authoritative state. Verify our serialization rules before adopting any debris/lifetime technique. |
| Capacity is shared with transient gameplay and physical placement includes sector and insertion conventions. | [P57](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p57-entitypool.html), [P71](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen2/p71-crab-factory.html) | Budget projectiles, debris and drops alongside persistent objects. Check actual occupancy rather than only configured pool maxima. For original conversion/pooling work, compare authored placement with recreated placement under nonzero insertion offsets and multiple sectors. |
| Resource closure extends beyond objects explicitly placed in the editor. | [P50](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p50-walker-yard.html), [P52](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p52-dark-jedi-gauntlet.html), [P61](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p61-image-menu.html) | SED/package checks should follow template inheritance and template-valued symbols in class and placed COGs. Record hard-coded engine dependencies separately; lexical closure alone cannot prove every feature is runnable. |
| A direct message into a script can bypass the interaction that a human must perform. | [P54](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p54-holodeck.html) | Keep injected-event tests for handler logic and add actual input, distance, facing and obstruction checks for reachable switches. A handler pass cannot certify a usable panel. |
| Geometry, physical traversal and visual quality are separate outcomes. | [P77](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen2/p77-airena.html), [P39](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p39-control-room.html) | Favor structural JKL geometry for major rooms and appropriate 3DO detail. Check actual engine lighting, sky and silhouette after mechanical tests pass. Keep source-render images labeled as source renders. |
| Editor consistency alone can miss portal and texture defects. | [P42](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p42-gothic-keep-redux.html), [P45](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p45-lathe-loops.html), [P53](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p53-campaign.html) | Reopen the exported JKL and compare both portal outlines, not just corner matching. Use asymmetric text/arrow fixtures to reveal mirrored or inverted UVs. Pin an editor operation to one process instead of a generic desktop window name. |
| Animation compatibility includes node identity, rest/evaluated pose, mesh budgets and sound allocation. | [P65](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen1/p65-motion-study.html), [P76](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen2/p76-f20ip-skins.html) | For original models, validate index/parent contracts and weapon-node reservations, then sample meaningful stock animations and inspect seams and grips. Preserve original sound IDs and test sound/SND preload capacity. A successful import/export is not proof that node order and pivots survived. |
| Restore the previous environment, not an imagined default. | [P74](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen2/p74-scuba.html) | Temporary effects should save ownership-relevant flags, model and camera state, and restore only what they own. Where stock COG cannot read an attribute, make the pinned-map dependency explicit instead of claiming arbitrary-map restoration. |
| Independent peers, state recovery and a deliberate failure distinguish a protocol from a local demonstration. | [P73](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen2/p73-network-cogs.html), [P74](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen2/p74-scuba.html) | A multiplayer acceptance test should establish joining first, then assert the intended failure under dropped, corrupted or misrouted messages. Test late joins and resynchronization from state. An expected missing-resend failure must remain a limitation. |
| Component identity matters across scale conversions and authority changes. | [P81](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen2/p81-space-scrap.html), [P82](https://smsithlord.com/openjkdf2libretro/cogfactory/gen2/p82-mind-wave.html) | Scale position, velocity, camera and audio ranges coherently; deliberately oversized effects need explicit gameplay rationale. Keep stable content IDs distinct from runtime indices and connection identity. P82 is a standalone JavaScript experiment, not a tested replacement for JK networking. |

## Historical design lessons

The older work adds design judgment that automated checks cannot supply. [Barn Battles IV](https://blog.smsithlord.com/dark-forces-ii-jedi-knight/barnbattles/) describes compact duels where sound helps locate an opponent outside sight. [Illusions Map Pack](https://blog.smsithlord.com/dark-forces-ii-jedi-knight/illusions/) varies movement choices between open floors, narrow catwalks and risky pickups. [SM HQ](https://blog.smsithlord.com/dark-forces-ii-jedi-knight/smhq/) uses selectable spawn areas to keep a large social/combat level useful for different group sizes. These suggest original map review questions: can a player read nearby action, do routes support different tactics, and can a sparse population find each other? They do not justify reproducing the author's layouts.

[Smoke Rocks](https://blog.smsithlord.com/dark-forces-ii-jedi-knight/smokerocks/) describes modular object architecture with custom LOD behavior and an unofficial-patch requirement. Together with P77's later lighting critique, this argues for selecting geometry by the actual rendering, lighting and compatibility contract rather than adopting a universal all-3DO rule. [Asylum of Mortality](https://blog.smsithlord.com/dark-forces-ii-jedi-knight/asylum-of-mortality/) makes destruction and height control central to the fight; the transferable lesson is to test the changed route and combat consequences, not only the break animation.

The public AArcade/OpenJKDF2 and Source Model Viewer summaries broaden the scope to model adoption and cross-engine placement. Their reported scale/orientation comparisons are useful test ideas, but they do not establish that our importers match those results. The [public status feed](https://smsithlord.com/site_content/status/status.json) also records a later upstream input fix expected to address P69's direct-boot hotkey problem. That older issue must be checked against the current OpenMotS revision before proposing a duplicate engine patch.

The [Jerec browser demo](https://smsithlord.com/jerec) is a PlayCanvas side-scroller, with animation/input and teleport scripts rather than JK COGs. Its scene references a Mixamo hierarchy; that alone establishes no retail skeleton compatibility. Home's AArcade exhibit uses small PlayCanvas components for scrolling/clamping texture maps and smoothing a light over elapsed time. Their useful general lesson is to distinguish an instance's mutable presentation from shared assets and to make timing explicit. These static observations are not an engine port or a demonstrated browser behavior, and no source code was imported.

Where his pages credit Raynar, Hell Raiser, ShadowX, EAH_CL0wN, Gnarly, Quaternius or others, preserve that chain if the relevant technique or asset is actually used. Implementation, local verification, GitLab publication and portfolio deployment are separate records; this research report alone claims none of them.
